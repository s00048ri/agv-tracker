AGV Tracker's first public release: a version-controlled dataset of the
venues that govern AI, plus the site and the pipeline that keep it moving.

**Live site**: <https://agv-tracker.pages.dev>

## What's in it

**118 AI governance venues**, 2015–2026, classified under **AGVO v0.3** —
six orthogonal dimensions (entity type, governance modality, topic focus,
geographic scope, lead actor, legal character) designed for a population
that spans standing IGO secretariats, treaty bodies, standards working
groups, recurring conferences and one-off summits alike.

Every row is **human-verified**: `human_verified_at` and
`human_verified_fields` are populated on all 118, backed by 484 rows in
`agv_evidence.csv` carrying per-field source attribution. 455 of 472
field-level confidence ratings are `high`, 3 are `low`. Relations,
renamings and lifecycle transitions live in their own tables
(`agv_relation.csv`, `agv_name_history.csv`, `agv_lifecycle.csv`) rather
than being flattened into the master table.

Alongside the data: a documented ontology (`docs/codebook.md`), a
hand-coding manual for reviewers (`docs/coding_manual.md`), a three-layer
source registry separating discovery from verification from background
literature, a static dashboard with three sensitivity views, per-venue
detail pages with full evidence panels, and a monthly
fetch → normalize → diff → PR pipeline that surfaces conflicts with
human-verified values rather than overwriting them.

## What it is not, yet

This is **v0.3, not v1.0**, and the gaps are worth stating plainly.

- **The discovery fetchers do not yet work against live sites.** Five of
  seven match nothing; their fixtures were authored to fit the selectors
  rather than captured from the sources. The monthly pipeline runs end to
  end and currently proposes nothing. `sources/registry.yml` records the
  status of each fetcher.
- **Coverage is uneven across entity types.** Four of thirteen have ten or
  more venues; `industry_conference` has four. The v1.0 target is ten
  each.
- **Data freshness lags.** `last_observed_activity_date` clusters around
  2026-03/04, and the differ flags 67 of 118 rows as stale candidates
  under the §3.5 thresholds. That is a refresh backlog, not a threshold
  problem.
- **The seed is not statistically representative.** It is a stress test of
  the ontology against real heterogeneity, and should be read as such.
